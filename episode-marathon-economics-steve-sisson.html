Paying to Suffer: The Strange Economics of Marathons with Special Guest Steve Sisson

October 12, 2026 • 59 minutes

Steve Sisson of Telos Running joins Jennifer and Julie to explore marathon costs, destination races and why runners pay to push their limits. Drink of choice: Michelob Ultra.

About This Episode

Running is supposed to be the cheap sport. So how did we end up with expensive shoes, destination-race weekends, and a credit card bill that needs its own recovery plan?

This week on Money Well Studio, Jennifer and Julie welcome running coach legend, Steve Sisson of Telos Running, to unpack the economics of marathons—and the even stranger psychology of people who keep signing up for them.

We follow the money from carbon-plated shoes and race entries to hotel rooms, road closures, charity fundraising, and the finish-line beer tent. Steve explains why runners happily pay for the privilege of suffering, how a race bib becomes permission to travel, and why reducing months of training to a single finish time can rob us of the best part of running.

Along the way, British royalty gets blamed for the final miserable 0.2 miles, Jennifer makes the case for bacon and mimosas at mile nine, and Steve delivers a spectacularly unfavorable review of our featured beverage, Michelob Ultra.

Come for the marathon math. Stay for the reminder that the return on all those miles might be a bigger life, rather than a faster time.
